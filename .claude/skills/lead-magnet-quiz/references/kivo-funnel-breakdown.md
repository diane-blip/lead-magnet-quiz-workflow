# Kivo Walking Quiz Funnel Breakdown

Reference funnel: https://quiz.kivo-walking.com/asian-walking

This is a high-converting quiz funnel targeting menopausal women. Each step has specific psychological purpose.

---

## Ad Strategy

**Format:** Conversational, third-party framing

**Example ad copy:**
```
"Wow, you're in incredible shape. What's your routine?"
"Honestly, I don't have a routine like that."
"How do you stay like this? Gym?"
"Um, no gym at all. I'm busy, work, family life. That's why I do Asian walking. Just nine minutes a day and I'm finally seeing my body tone up again."
```

**Why it works:**
- Conversation format = viewer feels like they're overhearing, not being sold to
- Handles biggest objection immediately ("I'm busy, work, family life")
- Transformation is specific but achievable
- Specificity creates believability

**Variations target different emotional hooks:**
- "This is day one of my stepmom doing Asian walking. This is day 28."
- "My wife wants to get toned before our daughter's wedding. What happens if she starts tomorrow?"

**Positioning pillars hammered repeatedly:**
- "9 minutes a day"
- "No gym, no equipment"

## Quiz Flow (Step by Step)

### Step 1: Identity-First Segmentation
**Ask:** Age bracket selection

**Why:**
- Non-threatening (zero cognitive load)
- Immediately segments for personalization
- Image shows fit, confident woman in target demo (achievable, not intimidating)
- Proof that "women like you" can look like this

---

### Step 2: Goal-Based Commitment
**Ask:** Select your goal (Lose weight, etc.)

**Psychology:**
- First micro-commitment
- Clicking "Lose weight" = identity statement: "I am someone who wants to lose weight"
- Commitment and consistency principle kicks in
- Everything that follows speaks to THAT person

---

### Step 3: Belief Reinforcement (No Data Collection)
**Show:** "Just 7 minutes a day boost your physical health"

**Why this exists:**
- Zero data collection on this screen
- It's a belief-building checkpoint
- After commitment, reinforce they made the right choice
- Copy mentions "menopause" = mirroring identity back to them

---

### Step 4: Visual Self-Identification
**Ask:** Select body type from images

**Psychology:**
- Forces admission: clicking "Plus-sized" = admitting something to themselves
- That admission creates psychological pressure toward change
- YOUR quiz becomes the vehicle for that change
- Images are neutral, realistic, not judgmental
- Truth-telling creates ownership

---

### Step 5: Temporal Pain Point
**Ask:** When did you feel your best?

**Why it's brilliant:**
- Forces prospect to quantify their decline
- "More than 5 years ago" hurts to admit
- Creates emotional gap: "I used to be better, something went wrong"
- That gap is what your product fills
- "Never" option catches people excluded by transformation messaging

---

### Step 6: Problem Area Specificity
**Ask:** Select problem areas (belly, arms, etc.)

**Purpose:**
- Creates specificity ("belly fat" more real than "weight")
- Creates ownership (pointing at THEIR problems)
- "Improve whole body" toggle = easy out while still capturing high-intent

---

### Step 7: Data Collection with Real-Time Feedback
**Ask:** Current weight
**Show:** BMI calculation immediately

**Example output:**
"Your BMI is 27.8 which is considered overweight. You have some work ahead of you, but it's great that you're taking this first step."

**Why:**
- Data collection disguised as value delivery
- Creates a "gap" (you're overweight)
- Validates their decision (good first step)
- Promises personalization (program just for you)
- Softened language ("some work ahead" not "you're fat")
- Problem feels real but solvable

---

### Step 8: Goal Weight with Benefit Framing
**Show:** "Lose 17% of your weight"
**Include:** Health benefits (reduced heart attack risk, lower blood sugar)

**Why:**
- Transforms weight loss from vanity to health
- "Reduced chance of heart attack" > "look better"
- Validates their goal
- Specific percentage (17%) feels calculated, not arbitrary

---

### Step 9: Reframing Conventional Wisdom
**Show:** "Forget 10,000 steps. Starting small is how all change begins."

**Purpose:**
- Objection handling disguised as motivation
- Removes barrier before it becomes reason to quit
- "Starting small" echoes "9 minutes a day" positioning from ads

---

### Step 10: Loading State with Social Proof
**Show:** Progress bar + testimonials

**Elements:**
- "395,000 people have chosen Kivo"
- Testimonial: "I used to overcomplicate fitness. Now I just follow the plan."

**Why loading screens matter:**
- Social proof during anticipation
- Testimonials handle objections
- Let excitement build

**Testimonial hits:**
- "Overcomplicate fitness" (this is simple)
- "Just follow the plan" (no thinking required)
- "It actually works" (results)
- "Mood is better" (unexpected benefit)

---

### Step 11: Personalized Profile Reveal
**Show:** Complete profile with all their data

**Elements:**
- Visual BMI gauge
- Body type label ("Mesomorph")
- Experience level ("Intermediate")

**Why:**
- Data dump makes everything feel personalized
- Presented as "profile" like medical chart
- They can SEE where they are vs where they should be
- Labels give positive identity markers

---

### Step 12: Transformation Visualization with Specific Date
**Show:** "By September 20, 2026 you'll be at 75 kg"

**Key elements:**
- Specific date (not "someday")
- References emotional anchor captured earlier ("the wedding")

**Why:**
- Future pacing at its most specific
- Specific date feels like a forecast
- "Lose 15kg" is abstract
- "Lose 15kg by the wedding" is a story

---

### Step 13: Pricing Page (Full Trigger Stack)

**Elements:**

1. **Urgency:** Countdown timer ("up to 75% discount, 14:38 remaining")

2. **Before/After comparison:**
   - Now: Body fat 34-38%, Healthy weight (partial)
   - Goal: Body fat 29-33%, Healthy weight (full)
   - Visual progress bars show gap and achievable state

3. **Daily cost reframe:**
   - "$0.33 per day" instead of "$9.99/month"
   - Less than any coffee or impulse purchase

4. **Social proof at purchase:**
   - "People using the plan for 3 months achieve double the results"
   - Pushes toward longer commitment while framing as helpful advice

## Key Patterns to Apply

### Question Design
1. Start with identity (low friction)
2. Get goal commitment early
3. Include belief-building screens (no data, just reinforcement)
4. Use visual self-identification for ownership
5. Quantify decline to create emotional gap
6. Collect data while delivering value (BMI, scores)

### Copy Principles
- Mirror their identity back to them
- Soften hard truths ("some work ahead" not "you're fat")
- Transform vanity goals into health goals
- Handle objections as "reframes" not defensiveness
- Use specific dates, not vague timeframes

### Social Proof Placement
- In ads (transformation stories)
- During loading (testimonials)
- At purchase (usage stats, results data)

### Pricing Psychology
- Daily cost reframe
- Before/after visual comparison
- Urgency (countdown)
- Longer commitment = more results (not just cheaper)
